# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make food using light energy.

Plants mainly carry out photosynthesis in their leaves. The green pigment chlorophyll absorbs light energy from sunlight.

## What plants need

Plants need three main inputs for photosynthesis:

- carbon dioxide from the air,
- water absorbed through the roots,
- light energy from the Sun.

## What happens

Water travels from the roots to the leaves.

Carbon dioxide enters the leaves through tiny openings called stomata.

Chlorophyll captures light energy.

The plant uses this energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose.

Glucose is a sugar that stores chemical energy and can be used by the plant for growth and other life processes.

Oxygen is produced and released into the air.

## Word equation

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Light energy and chlorophyll are required for this process.

## Why photosynthesis matters

Photosynthesis provides food energy for plants.

Animals and humans depend directly or indirectly on plants for food.

Photosynthesis also releases oxygen into the atmosphere.

## Common misconception

Plants do not obtain their food directly from soil.

Plants absorb water and minerals from soil, but they make glucose through photosynthesis.
